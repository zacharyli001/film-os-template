# Story Design Lenses

These lenses are diagnostic choices, not a compulsory structure.

## Dramatic engine

Define the active pressure in plain language:

- Who is trying to cause or prevent what now?
- What force makes the old equilibrium impossible?
- Why can the central conflict not be solved by one honest conversation or one easy exit?
- What becomes more costly, irreversible, or revealing as the story continues?

A strong premise can still support a quiet, fragmentary, anti-climactic, or ensemble form. Do not confuse low spectacle with low dramatic pressure.

## Character design

For each central character, distinguish:

- declared objective;
- behaviorally demonstrated need, fear, blind spot, or contradiction;
- resources and leverage;
- what they cannot admit, say, or do;
- the relationship or environment that keeps the conflict alive;
- plausible change, regression, refusal, or exposure.

Use models such as desire/need, wound/false belief, or stable contradiction only when they illuminate this specific character. Do not fill them as mandatory fields.

## Structure selection

Choose the lightest structure that makes causality and experience legible. Possible lenses include escalation and reversal, circular return, progressive collapse, parallel comparison, delayed disclosure, episodic accumulation, mood movement, or a deliberately withheld climax. State why the chosen shape serves the creator's intended experience.

## Opening promise

Test whether the opening establishes enough of the following without exposition overload:

- a distinctive image, behavior, tension, or question;
- the work's tone and contract with the audience;
- a pressure that invites continued attention;
- the viewpoint through which the audience will experience uncertainty.

A hook may be quiet. It needs specificity and forward curiosity, not mandatory intensity.

## Dual-track rhythm

Track two curves across sequences or episode movements:

- **external movement**: danger, progress, reversal, information, power, or consequence;
- **internal movement**: intimacy, shame, hope, alienation, grief, desire, or self-recognition.

The curves need not peak together. Purposeful misalignment often creates depth: public victory with private collapse, quiet action with intense internal consequence, or spectacle followed by delayed feeling.

## Scene plan

For every proposed scene record:

- its immediate dramatic question;
- who drives it and what resists them;
- information advantage or concealment;
- start value and end value, or why the lack of change is dramatically meaningful;
- entry pressure and exit consequence;
- estimated screen duration;
- setup, advancement, or payoff obligations;
- downstream production risk only when it affects story design.

Delete, combine, or redesign a scene that merely repeats known information unless repetition itself changes power, meaning, or emotion.
