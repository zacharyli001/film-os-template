# Series Development Lenses

Use these lenses to test a series, not to make it resemble a platform formula.

## Series engine

A viable continuing engine identifies:

- what repeatedly brings the ensemble into conflict;
- why the central relationships cannot simply dissolve;
- what varies from episode to episode;
- what accumulates or changes irreversibly across the season;
- what audience promise returns without repeating the same plot.

Stress-test the engine with at least three materially different episode areas. If every example is the premise event again, the engine is not yet proven.

## Two-layer architecture

Plan each episode on two levels:

- **episode contract**: its immediate promise, dramatic question, turn, consequence, and satisfying degree of closure;
- **series movement**: what changes in power, knowledge, relationship, identity, world state, or an active long thread.

An ending hook should grow from the episode's consequence. Do not attach an unrelated surprise solely to force continuation.

## Character network

Design conflicts as a network rather than isolated biographies. Track who needs whom, who controls which resource or truth, whose objectives collide, and where alliances create costs elsewhere. Secondary characters need their own leverage, private logic, and movement; they are not delivery systems for the protagonist's plot.

## Arc and regression

Allocate meaningful movement across the season while allowing concealment, relapse, false progress, and re-interpretation. Treat numeric arc percentages as rough diagnostic signals only. The important question is whether each episode changes the audience's understanding or the character's available choices.

## Information release

For each significant fact, track:

- whether it is canon, suspicion, mystery, lie, dramatic irony, or future proposal;
- which characters know, believe, doubt, or misunderstand it;
- when the audience learns it;
- what action the release enables or prevents;
- whether the fact belongs to this episode's active context.

Reveal world rules through consequence, behavior, environment, and conflict before explanatory summary when possible.

## Thread and payoff planning

Classify threads by expected distance: same episode, near-term episodes, season-scale, or later-series possibility. A setup remains a proposal until approved and must not demand a payoff that the creator has not chosen. Avoid maintaining so many active subplots that no thread can materially move.

## Rhythm and duration

Map external and internal intensity per episode movement. Vary episode shapes when the series promise allows: pressure escalation, bottle/container episode, relationship chamber piece, investigation, aftermath, parallel paths, or another creator-approved form. Estimate scene and line durations against the target episode runtime; do not solve excess by making everyone speak unnaturally fast.

## Development checkpoint

At the end of a planning session, provide a compact checkpoint generated from canonical records:

- locked human decisions;
- open creative decisions;
- current season and episode states;
- active character/relationship changes;
- active mysteries and who knows what;
- planted, advanced, and paid-off threads;
- runtime pressure;
- next dependency.

This checkpoint is a readable view, not a second source of truth.
