# Screenplay Diagnostic Lenses

Use only the lenses relevant to the reported problem. A script is not defective because it declines a familiar formula.

## Diagnosis order

1. Restate the creator's intended audience experience and locked choices.
2. Identify the observable symptom with scene or page evidence.
3. Locate the likely cause at the highest responsible layer.
4. Show what downstream material the issue affects.
5. Propose the smallest repair and one alternative when the tradeoff is meaningful.
6. Distinguish craft repair from a new creative direction.

Do not polish dialogue to hide a missing scene purpose, rewrite scenes to hide a broken causal chain, or alter the protagonist to solve a production inconvenience.

## Structural lens

Check whether events cause later choices and consequences; escalation changes the problem rather than repeats it; reveals arrive after sufficient preparation; the ending resolves or deliberately reframes the work's active question; and setups/payoffs change meaning rather than behave as inventory.

If using a named structural model, use it as a comparison lens and explain the mismatch. Never mark a beat missing solely because the model expects it.

## Character lens

Check agency, contradiction, leverage, cost, relationship pressure, evolving tactics, and the difference between what the character wants and what their behavior reveals. A stable or regressing character may be intentional; diagnose whether the script makes that choice legible.

## Scene lens

Check dramatic objective and resistance, information advantage, value movement, entry/exit, duration, repetition, and consequence. A scene may perform multiple jobs, but it still needs one dominant dramatic reason to exist.

## Dialogue lens

Check whether speech acts on another person, voices remain distinguishable, exposition has conflict or cost, subtext remains playable, and silence or interruption is earned. Flag overly complete, explanatory, thematic, or uniformly eloquent speech without treating roughness or ambiguity as errors.

## Dual-track rhythm lens

Map external and internal intensity separately. Look for unintended monotony, constant synchronized peaks, missing recovery, emotional consequence arriving too early or never arriving, and episode turns that advance plot while leaving relationships unchanged.

## Production lens

Identify genuine generative-film risks: excessive unique assets, unstable identity/state changes, unmotivated location churn, impossible duration, crowded simultaneous action, dependency-heavy physical interaction, or continuity that cannot be represented explicitly. Report the creative cost of simplification. Production convenience does not automatically outrank the approved story.

## Repair brief

Each proposed repair states:

- primary variable;
- scenes affected;
- qualities to preserve;
- intended improvement;
- creative tradeoff;
- authority required;
- verification method.
